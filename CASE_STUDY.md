\## 2. Qualitative Insights \& User Research

\### Psychological Association with "Ghar Ka Khana"

\* Key associations: \*Mom, Oil Used, Hot Roti, Dal Chawal, Ghee, Desi, Quality Simple Food.\*



\### Existing Solutions \& User Behaviors (Sample Size Observations)

\* \*\*Maid:\*\* 13/14 living in flats hired a maid. 10/14 never cooked for themselves even if the maid was on holiday. Maid is also responsible for flat cleaning.

\* \*\*Dabba Service:\*\* 3/3 who tried Dibba services quit within a week and hired a maid.

\* \*\*Work Routine:\*\* 17/23 have variable routines (hybrid WFH/office setup) and don't go to office regularly.

\* \*\*PG/Hostel:\*\* 9/23 lived in PGs with no kitchen setup.



\### Reasons Cited for Shifting from Dabba System to Maid

1\. Too Spicy food / High masala sabzi

2\. Poorly cooked / hard rotis

3\. Small meal sizes

4\. Lack of choice

5\. No cutlery provided



\### What is the True Problem?

People lack access to quality, affordable home-cooked meals.

\* Cannot prepare on their own due to lack of energy, busy/variable routines, or poor cooking skills.

\* Hiring personal home cooks is cost-prohibitive for everyone.

\* Lack of trust in dabbawalas and mess food for daily healthy consumption.



\### Target User Demographics \& Prioritization

\* \*\*Respondents Profile:\*\* 20–35 year olds from North Indian states living away from home in Metro/Tier-1/2/3 cities (Delhi-NCR, Bengaluru, Pune, Mumbai).

\* \*\*Demographic Breakdown:\*\*

&#x20; \* 5–15 Yr: School going kids (Lunchbox)

&#x20; \* 15–20 Yr: Coaching + College students

&#x20; \* \*\*20–25 Yr: Bachelor Working Professionals living away from home (PRIORITIZED SEGMENT)\*\*

&#x20; \* 25–30 Yr: Young working married couples living away from home

\## 4. Supply Side: Home Chefs \& Unit Economics

\### Home Chef Segmentation

\* \*\*Rich / Upper Middle Class (25-50 yrs):\*\* Passionate about cooking.

\* \*\*Middle / Lower Income (20-50 yrs):\*\* Seeking extra income.



\### Chef Model Comparison (MVP Selection)

\* \*\*Situation 1: Cloud Kitchens by Passionate Women\*\*

&#x20; \* \*Investment:\* High (Real estate, hiring)

&#x20; \* \*Scale:\* Small (\~25k women across 8 metros)

&#x20; \* \*Logistics:\* Easy (250 kitchens)

\* \*\*Situation 2: Women Serving from Households (PRIORITIZED FOR MVP)\*\*

&#x20; \* \*Investment:\* Low (Basic ingredients \& standard utensils)

&#x20; \* \*Scale:\* Huge (\~1.75 Cr women across 8 metros)

&#x20; \* \*Logistics:\* Complex (5,000 kitchens)



\### Key Supply Barriers

1\. Difficulty in acquiring and convincing customers.

2\. Inability to handle last-mile delivery independently.

3\. Lack of capital for kitchen upgrades or raw material procurement.



\### Unit Economics (Per Home Chef)

\* \*\*Demand per Slot:\*\* 10–15 meals.

\* \*\*Customer AOV (3 meals/day - Morning, Lunch, Dinner):\*\* ₹250 – ₹350

\* \*\*Chef's Variable Cost (3 meals):\*\* ₹80 – ₹120

\* \*\*Delivery Agent Fee (3 meals):\*\* ₹60

\* \*\*Zomato Commission (20%):\*\* ₹50 – ₹70

\* \*\*Chef's Net Profit per 3 Meals:\*\* ₹60 – ₹100

\* \*\*Chef's Daily Earnings:\*\* ₹600 – ₹1,500

\## 5. Solution Positioning, System Architecture \& Operational Flow

\### Positioning Matrix

\* \*\*Axes:\*\* Trust on Quality (Low to High) vs. Experience (Non-Home to Home).

\* \*\*Positioning:\*\* Zomato Mom sits in the \*\*High Trust + High Home Experience\*\* quadrant (outperforming Mess, Cafeterias, Caterers, and Dabbawalas).



\### Raw Material Quality Assurance (Blinkit Integration)

\* \*\*Mechanism:\*\* Chef partners obtain a \*"Zomato Verified Ingredients"\* tag by ordering ingredients via Blinkit.

\* \*\*Verification Rule:\*\* Blinkit order logs (items, qty, date) are cross-checked with Zomato order volume. A $>25\\%$ mismatch triggers a physical audit inspection.

\* \*\*System Flow:\*\*

&#x20; `Customer App` $\\rightarrow$ `Load Balancer` $\\rightarrow$ `Application Server` $\\leftrightarrow$ `Zomato DB (Customer/Chef Data)` \& `Blinkit DB (Raw Material Data)`.



\### Ecosystem Workflow

1\. \*\*Awareness:\*\* User discovers Zomato Mom on Zomato app $\\rightarrow$ Chooses chef $\\rightarrow$ Schedules delivery/subscription.

2\. \*\*Raw Material Fulfillment:\*\* Chef orders ingredients on Blinkit $\\rightarrow$ Blinkit feeds verification data to Zomato.

3\. \*\*Order Transmission:\*\* Zomato sends order details \& schedule to Chef.

4\. \*\*Pickup \& Delivery:\*\* Delivery partner assigned $\\rightarrow$ Picks food from Home Chef $\\rightarrow$ Delivers hot meal to Customer.

\## 8. Risk \& Mitigation Matrix

\* \*\*Desirability Risk:\*\*

&#x20; \* \*Risk:\* Capacity constraints of popular chefs; lack of interest in subscriptions.

&#x20; \* \*Mitigation:\* Incentivize chefs in nearby high-demand zones; set up Zomato-run cloud kitchens in high-demand clusters with a full-stack model (Real estate, equipment, chef hiring managed by Zomato).

\* \*\*Feasibility Risk:\*\*

&#x20; \* \*Risk:\* Cold food due to travel delays; unverified ingredients; Blinkit integration friction.

&#x20; \* \*Mitigation:\* Strict delivery radius limits; automated Blinkit ingredient verification delta checks.

\* \*\*Viability Risk:\*\*

&#x20; \* \*Risk:\* Unfavorable unit economics for chefs; high Chef Acquisition Cost (CAC).

&#x20; \* \*Mitigation:\* Bulk procurement discount via Blinkit; optimized hyper-local delivery route pooling.
